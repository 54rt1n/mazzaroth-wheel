# Historical method for the Three Stars Each explorer

## What the wheel represents

Use **Astrolabe B, the monthly list (section III; also called section C in older discussions)** as the dataset identity. It assigns one entry in each of the Ea, Anu, and Enlil paths to each of twelve months. The preceding three-column star catalogue is a different arrangement; MUL.APIN is another text, not a substitute list of 36. Section IV repeats the monthly triads with their six-month counterparts. [1]

The source tablet is **VAT 9416 = KAV 218 = CDLI P281769**, excavated at Assur and held in Berlin. CDLI classifies it as Middle Assyrian; Cifola and Hughey, following Horowitz, date it to the early-to-middle twelfth century BCE and describe its writer as a Babylonian scribe at the Assyrian court. [1–2]

Recommended persistent label: **“36 monthly entries · three celestial paths · schematic calendar.”** This is neither a catalogue of 36 equal zodiac sectors nor the Egyptian decan scheme. If an actual sky projection is also provided, identify that as a separate modern reconstruction.

## Display rules

- The wheel’s angle represents the listed month; its rings represent the listed path. These are not measured ecliptic longitudes or declinations.
- Retain the tablet’s path assignment even where a proposed identification seems geographically inconsistent. EN.TE.NA.BAR.ḪUM, conventionally associated with Centaurus, occurs in the Enlil path in month VII. Reiner and Pingree discuss both mythological and astronomical considerations; the transmitted paths should not be rendered as established, precisely bounded declination bands. [9, pp. 3–4]
- Label a modern star or constellation identification as secure, probable, disputed, or unknown. The source name and proposed modern referent are separate fields.
- Preserve damaged readings, alternative readings, and edition differences explicitly. Do not silently merge an older Pinches-type chart with Astrolabe B.
- Do not assign a fixed Gregorian date to a Babylonian month. A seasonal gloss can be approximate; a specific ancient observation date requires an epoch and calendar reconstruction.
- Keep **original referent** separate from **later association**. Four transmitted names have familiar later planetary associations: **Dilbat → Venus (I, Anu), Šulpae → Jupiter (IV, Enlil), Ṣalbatānu → Mars (IX, Ea), Marduk → Jupiter (XII, Anu)**. Their referents in this earlier monthly list remain unresolved here. Do not classify these slots as proven planets, infer duplicate original objects from the later Jupiter association, or substitute planetary coordinates. Reiner and Pingree themselves hypothesize that older stellar names were subsequently used for planets; Table II's familiar identifications do not settle that historical question. [9, pp. 1–4]
- The tensions between later identifications and the list's recurring seasonal structure are evidence to investigate. Cifola and Hughey explicitly question the backward application of later planetary and fixed-star identifications. Test possible changes of referent, textual history, and interpretations of rising/setting before inferring an error by the compilers. Neither original planetary identity nor an original fixed-star identity has been established for these unresolved slots. [1, especially §9]
- A modern catalogue marker locates a proposed referent. It does not reconstruct the original constellation boundary or guarantee that a name retained the same astronomical referent across periods. Unresolved entries should retain their transmitted calendar positions without an invented sky coordinate.

## Brief research note for the UI

**A possible reading system.** Cifola and Hughey (2023) propose that the monthly triads served as visible reminders of seasonal duties and divine messages. They connect this “heavenly writing” with Marduk’s ordered cosmos and suggest a narrative framework. This is a published interpretation of the list, not a demonstrated Median Magian mnemonic system. [1, §§8–9]

| **Ṣalbatānu**, later associated with Mars | No secure name in Job 38. | Middle Persian **Wahrām — Mars**. | **Later planetary comparison (conditional):** same unresolved original referent and chronological qualification. [8–9] |
| **Šulpae / Marduk**, later associated with Jupiter | No secure name in Job 38. | Middle Persian **Ohrmazd — Jupiter**. | **Later planetary comparison (conditional):** same qualification. This does not prove the two early slots had one astronomical referent. Distinguish the planetary usage from the deity's name. [8–9] |

**Mazzaroth** is a comparison for the *system*, not a securely identified point. Its relationship to **mazzalot** and Akkadian **manzaltu** is a scholarly proposal; the 36 monthly stars are one suggested interpretation. Do not title the reconstruction “the recovered Mazzaroth” or present a complete shared story as established. [5]
